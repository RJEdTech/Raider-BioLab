Course level
For teachers: how this works

One simulation, three levels

The course-level switch keeps the same model running underneath and changes what students can see and control.

  • Bio gives the core variables and plain-language labels.
  • Honors adds named structures, inhibitors and a second variable to compare.
  • AP adds the quantitative readouts: proton-motive force, P/O ratio, Vmax and Km, gross vs. net O₂.

A 15-minute lesson shape

  • Students run the predict-then-test challenges first, committing to an answer before they see it.
  • Then they design their own trial: change one variable, record trials, and plot them on the Trials graph.
  • Copy the table as CSV into Excel or Desmos for analysis or a lab write-up.

About the models

Every number on screen is calculated from a model, not played back from an animation, so unexpected combinations of settings still behave correctly. The models are simplified on purpose:

  • Respiration: pumps fill an H⁺ gradient and ATP synthase drains it. NADH pumps 10 H⁺, FADH₂ pumps 6, and each ATP costs about 4 H⁺. Rates are per mitochondrion, in model units.
  • Enzymes: Michaelis–Menten kinetics with temperature, pH and inhibitor effects. Substrate is held constant. Heating above about 55 °C damages the enzyme permanently until you press Reset.
  • Photosynthesis: light falls off with the square of lamp distance. Bubbles are counted over the last minute and vary randomly, like real data.

Privacy

Everything runs in the browser. No logins, and no student data leaves the device.
