For teachers: how this works

One site, three levels of detail

The Detail switch keeps the same model running underneath. It changes how much students see and control, and any student can choose any level:

  • Big picture (fits Biology): the main idea in everyday words, with the core variables.
  • Closer look (fits Honors Biology): the parts get their names, with more variables and more challenges.
  • Deep dive (fits AP Biology and AP Environmental Science): everything, including the numbers, the math and claim–evidence–reasoning prompts.

Built to make the invisible concrete

  • Walk me through it: a guided, slowed-down tour that dims everything except the part being explained.
  • Slow motion and Step: speeds down to 0.1×, and Step moves forward a moment at a time while paused.
  • Live caption: explains what is happening right now, and why.
  • Scale line: tells students where in the body they are and how big it is.

Three ways in

  • Guided (every tool opens here): the walkthrough one step at a time, then the predict-then-test questions one at a time.
  • Challenge (on some tools): a scored game where students calculate the answer first, like landing a projectile on a target.
  • Full lab: every variable, the graph, the data table and an investigation.

Virtual labs: your handouts, on a virtual bench

Some tools have a Virtual lab button that runs an RJ lab handout step by step. Students read the instruments themselves and record their own data in the handout’s tables. They graph it, answer the handout’s questions, write the conclusion, and save the finished lab as a PDF or Word file to turn in on Canvas. Use it for absences, make-ups or pre-lab practice.

  • Catalase activity: yeast amount and temperature change (Biology) and the effects of pH on catalase (Honors Biology)
  • Leaf disk photosynthesis (Biology)
  • Mung bean germination (Biology, Unit 1)
  • Wisconsin Fast Plants (Honors Biology) and chi-square analysis with a bag of candies (AP Biology)
  • Respiration vs photosynthesis with CO₂ and O₂ sensors (Honors Biology) and respiration in germinating seeds (AP Biology)
  • Cemetery lab: survivorship curves (APES)
  • Fetal pig and grasshopper dissection with a practice practical (Honors Biology)

Put a tool in Canvas

Open any tool and use Embed this tool in Canvas at the bottom. Choose where it starts, copy the code and paste it into a Canvas page.

A 15-minute lesson shape

  • Students start with the walkthrough, then answer the predict-then-test challenges before they see each result.
  • Next, the Investigation box above each data table turns the simulation into a lab: change one variable, record trials, and plot them.
  • Copy as CSV moves the data into Excel or Desmos for a write-up.

About the models

Numbers come from simplified models, not recorded animations, so unexpected settings still behave sensibly. Each simulation’s “About this model” section lists its assumptions.

Privacy

Everything runs in the browser. There are no logins, and no student data leaves the device.
